# Lesson 006 — Lift the octave. Save the solo.

[MIDI download](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/compositionhacked/lessons/main/006-lift-the-octave-before-the-solo/006-demonstration.mid) · [MP3 download](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/compositionhacked/lessons/main/006-lift-the-octave-before-the-solo/006-demonstration.mp3)

A practical lesson from the supplied Purple Rain composition method, Book I Lesson 15, on the title song. Its transferable device is a staged climax: establish a motif, lift its register, then let a new lead line arrive later. The music here is newly composed in D major, not a transcription, performance or reharmonization of Prince's melody. All teaching audio is synthesized.

## Hear the change

60 seconds, 80 BPM, 4/4. One four-bar loop: D | Bm | G | A, one chord per bar. The last A deliberately points back to D when you loop it; the file is a demonstration, not a finished song ending.

| Time | Bars | What changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–12 s | 1–4 | A: compact eight-note motif |
| 12–24 s | 5–8 | B: identical motif, every pitch one octave higher |
| 24–36 s | 9–12 | Repeat B while inspecting the note map |
| 36–48 s | 13–16 | A new lead replaces the motif, with a new rhythm and longer sustain |
| 48–60 s | 17–20 | Return to B under the twelve-bar assignment |

A and B use the same tempo, chord voicings, bass, drums, melody attack times, note lengths, velocities and synthesis patch. Only the motif pitches rise by 12 semitones. There is no new octave doubling, added melody note or extra distortion in B. The accompaniment stays in its original register. At 36 seconds we deliberately introduce a different line and a longer-sustain envelope: that is a new foreground role, not another controlled A/B comparison.

## Exact pitches and functions

Middle C = C4 (MIDI 60). Every compact/wide motif note starts on beat 1 or 3 and lasts 1.6 quarter-note beats (1.2 s). Eight attacks per four-bar pass in both A and B.

| Chord | Bass | Upper accompaniment, low to high | Compact motif, beats 1 / 3 | Wide motif, beats 1 / 3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D (I) | D2 (38) | D3 F♯3 A3 (50,54,57) | F♯4 / A4 (66,69) | F♯5 / A5 (78,81) |
| Bm (vi) | B1 (35) | D3 F♯3 B3 (50,54,59) | F♯4 / D4 (66,62) | F♯5 / D5 (78,74) |
| G (IV) | G1 (31) | B2 D3 G3 (47,50,55) | G4 / B4 (67,71) | G5 / B5 (79,83) |
| A (V) | A1 (33) | C♯3 E3 A3 (49,52,57) | E4 / C♯4 (64,61) | E5 / C♯5 (76,73) |

Bass and accompaniment attack beat 1 and last 3.8 beats. Their bass roots move D2–B1–G1–A1; there is no fixed bass pedal. The Bm, G and A upper shapes are inversions above separate root-position bass notes. The overall chords remain D, Bm, G and A.

Drums remain unchanged: kick on beats 1 and 3, snare on 2 and 4, closed hat every eighth note. The octave lift increases the distance between motif and accompaniment. It changes register and perceived scale without increasing attack density.

## The late lead

Only bars 13–16 contain this line. Format: beat, pitch, duration in beats.

- D: (1, F♯5, 1.0), (2&, E5, 0.5), (3&, F♯5, 1.25).
- Bm: (1, D5, 1.5), (3, F♯5, 1.5).
- G: (1, B5, 2.0), (3&, A5, 0.5), (4, G5, 0.75).
- A: (1, E5, 1.5), (3, D5, 0.6), (4, C♯5, 0.85).

E5 over D is a brief ninth, A5 over G a brief ninth. Over A, D5 is a melodic fourth that resolves down a semitone to the chord's third C♯5. The highest lead note B5 belongs to G major. No artist solo is quoted.

## Play or edit it

Import the Standard MIDI File with its 80 BPM tempo map. Six tracks: Conductor, Harmony, Bass, Motif, Lead and Drums. The MIDI and MP3 contain the same pitches, note lengths, velocities and section timing. Assign clean guitar patches to Motif/Lead, a soft pad to Harmony, finger bass to Bass and a GM kit on channel 10. Our custom synthesis differs from a stock GM patch; the Lead intentionally sustains longer. Every MIDI note releases before the intended 60-second ending.

For guitar, standard tuning E2 A2 D3 G3 B3 E4. One compact fingering, in phrase order (string/fret): B7, B10 | B7, G7 | B8, e7 | G9, G6. This gives F♯4 A4 | F♯4 D4 | G4 B4 | E4 C♯4. The wide version on the high e string is 14,17 | 14,10 | 15,19 | 12,9. Verify the octave by listening to each pair. Bass and pad are separate parts; no six-string shape is implied.

## Write twelve bars

Repeat D | Bm | G | A three times. Bars 1–4: write eight motif attacks. Bars 5–8: move every motif note up one octave; preserve attack times, durations and accompaniment. Bars 9–12: replace the motif with a lead answer. Give at least one sustained note a named chord-tone destination. Keep your master gain fixed while comparing versions. The supplied audio includes explanatory repetitions; your twelve-bar arrangement is the assignment, not a claim about the full form of Purple Rain.
