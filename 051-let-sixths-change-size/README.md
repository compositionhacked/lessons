## Hear the difference

The upper melody, attack times, durations, accents, backing, bass roots and instruments stay fixed. Version A deliberately puts a major sixth (nine semitones) below every melody note. Version B uses the diatonic pairs from bars 1–4 of the method’s original etude 6A, “Two Voices Walking.” Only the lower voice changes. A is a comparison added for this Reel, not the source etude.

Over D, the first three pairs in B are D4–B4, E4–C♯5 and F♯4–D5. Their spans are 9, 9 and 8 semitones. Making the last pair nine semitones instead gives F4–D5; F-natural conflicts with the backing’s F♯. A chromatic note can be intentional, but a fixed guitar shape should not choose it by accident. Over G and Em7, the B3–G4 pair is another minor sixth; the fixed-major-sixth control substitutes B♭3 below G4.

## Play it on guitar

Use the G and high-E strings, skipping and muting the B string. The following fret pairs are G-string / high-E-string; each sounds both voices together:

| Source bar | B fret pairs |
| --- | --- |
| D | 7/7, 9/9, 11/10, 9/9, 7/7 |

| Em7 | 4/3, 7/7, 9/9, 7/7, 4/3 |

For the A comparison, keep every high-E fret and place the G-string finger at that same fret: 7/7, 9/9, 10/10, etc. In particular, D’s third event changes from 10/10 in A to 11/10 in B. Standard tuning makes those F4–D5 and F♯4–D5, respectively. Pick the two strings with separate fingers. Practice broken intervals first, then pinch them together and damp both at the written rests.

## Write

Write one eight-note melody and harmonize it three ways: thirds, sixths, and a mixture. Keep the bass and rhythm identical. Choose by register and phrase shape. Then try interrupting one parallel passage with a held lower note or a single-note cadence. That final variation is a writing prompt; it is not performed in this demonstration. Check every note against the current bass, rather than treating all diatonic notes as equivalent chord tones.
