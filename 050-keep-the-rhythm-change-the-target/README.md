# Lesson 050 — Keep the rhythm. Change the target.

## The musical choice
A motif can remain recognizable while its chord targets change. First hear the same six-note phrase over all four chords; then hear the method's altered A7 phrase. All attack times, note lengths, accents, bass roots, chord voicings, percussion and timbres stay fixed. Only the lead pitches in bar 4 change.

| Bar | Chord / bass | Version A | Version B |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Dm / D2 | F4 A4 C5 A4, rest, F4 A4 | Same |
| 2 | B♭maj7 / B♭2 | F4 A4 C5 A4, rest, F4 A4 | Same |
| 3 | Gm7 / G2 | F4 A4 C5 A4, rest, F4 A4 | Same |
| 4 | A7 / A2 | F4 A4 C5 A4, rest, F4 A4 | E4 A4 C♯5 A4, rest, G4 E4 |

The six attacks fall on beats 1, 1&, 2, 2&, 3&, 4. Durations in quarter-note beats: 0.5, 0.5, 0.5, 0.5, 0.5, 1. Beat 3 is a written rest. The bass attacks beats 1 and 3 for 0.8 beat each, changing roots each bar; there is no fixed bass pedal.

Over Dm, F–A–C gives third, fifth, seventh. Over B♭maj7, those same notes become fifth, major seventh, ninth. Over Gm7, they become seventh, ninth, eleventh. These colors are deliberate. Over A7, C natural rubs against the backing's C♯. Version B uses E as the fifth, C♯ as the major third, and G as the seventh before ending on E. The phrase's rhythm and gap still identify the hook. This is a different compositional decision from automatically changing every non-chord tone.
