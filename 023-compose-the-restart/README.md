## Hear the device

The full pattern combines A2 pulse, C♯4–E4–A4 offbeats and a short E5–F♯5–E5–C♯5 riff. Compare four full bars with full band → bass alone → riff alone → full return. Layers are removed intentionally; no master gain jump creates the return.

This original teaching example is 120 BPM in 4/4, 48.000 seconds, 24 bars. It illustrates a transferable device rather than transcribing or explaining the whole recording. Notes use scientific pitch notation (middle C = C4, MIDI 60). The chromatic pitch display uses C♯, E♭, F♯, G♯ and B♭ spellings; the chord labels and explanation give their harmonic functions.

## Exact performance and DAW instructions

Import the Standard MIDI File at its embedded tempo. Five tracks contain conductor/markers, bass (channel 1), harmony (channel 2), lead (channel 3) and an optional guide part (channel 4). Empty parts are intentional. All notes in the MP3 occur in this MIDI, including the guide when present; no extra backing or hidden drone is added. Assign a short electric-bass sound, clean plucked harmony, a clear lead and a contrasting guide sound. The demonstration uses simple additive/plucked synthesis; General MIDI program hints do not reproduce its exact timbre.

Exact onset beats, duration in quarter-note beats, MIDI pitches, velocities and part names are in [note-events.json](./note-events.json). Every release is clamped before the 96-beat ending. Repeated short bass notes are separate attacks, not a sustained pedal waveform. Where a tonic pedal is described, it means the repeated bass pitch stays fixed.

Play at the specified octave. Standard guitar tuning is E2 A2 D3 G3 B3 E4, with no capo in these newly composed examples. Bass and high keyboard parts can be recorded separately; this is an arrangement score, not a claim that one guitar plays all tracks simultaneously. Keep the master gain unchanged between A and B.

## Write with it

Write a 16-bar ending on A and D/A. Use four bars full, four bass only, four riff only, then four full. Keep tempo fixed and give the final chord a planned cut.

Record your completed exercise, then remove or reverse the device and compare at the same master gain. The 24-bar demonstration includes explanatory repeats and a four-bar workshop preview. Your composition is a separate assignment.
