# 001 — One bass note. Three worlds.

One low E can support three different harmonic colours. The practical move is to hold that bass still and change the notes above it.

## Hear what actually changes

| E bass plus | Notes above it | What to hear |
| --- | --- | --- |
| E minor | E, G, B | G is the minor third. |
| E major | E, G#, B | Move only G to G#: the third changes the chord's identity. |
| F# major | F#, A#, C# | A# is the raised fourth against E; F# and C# supply the 9th and 6th. |

F#/E means an F# major triad above an E bass. In the E-centred context established by this demonstration, it supplies Lydian colour. E stays the reference; a different bass would change the interpretation.

Now write a phrase: hold A# over F#/E, then move it up to B while returning the upper chord to E major. The raised fourth resolves to the fifth. Hear that change before assigning it an emotional label.

## Play it on guitar

Standard tuning, strings low to high. `x` means mute.

| Sound | Frets |
| --- | --- |
| Em | `0 x 2 0 0 x` |
| E major | `0 x 2 1 0 x` |
| F#/E | `0 x 4 3 2 x` |

Keep the low E sounding. Use the same rhythm and tone in each version so you can isolate the harmonic effect.

## Write eight bars

1. Hold an E bass throughout.
2. Use two bars each of **Em → E → F#/E → E**.
3. Write one melodic idea around G becoming G#.
4. Write another around A# resolving to B.
5. Export your version. Then change the rhythm, register or instrument and notice what survives.

## MIDI guide

Import the `.mid` file into your DAW. It is Standard MIDI File format 1, with 480 ticks per quarter note, tempo and section markers. Assign a clean plucked sound to the upper triad, a bass to the bass tracks and any suitable lead to the melody. MIDI stores notes, timing and performance data; it does not contain the MP3 audio.

The Reel demonstrates each stage separately before the writing exercise. The workshop file gives you the complete eight-bar progression to edit.
