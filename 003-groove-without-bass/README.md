## The mechanism

A recurring D4 supplies a tonal anchor in the guitar register. It is short and reattacked, not a sustained pedal or hidden bass drone. F4–A4–D5 states a D-minor triad with F in the bottom of the upper shape. The single-note answer D5–C5–A4–E5–D5 adds the minor seventh C and ninth E around that triad. Dm9 describes the resulting harmonic field over time; all five notes are not sustained together as one chord. The harmony is static throughout.

Removing the bass changes register and weight. It does not remove the kick drum's low-frequency energy. The bass-free version contains no pitched instrument below D4 (MIDI 62). Its tonal center comes from recurrence, the minor triad and phrase endings. This is one arrangement choice, not a complete explanation of Prince's song.

## Write eight bars

Use Dm for all eight bars. Keep a short D anchor in the guitar register, a compact minor triad and the same drum groove. Use no bass instrument and no low synth replacement. Introduce one rhythmic change every two bars. Record a second version with a D2 bass part and compare both at the same master gain. Which version gives your melody more room? The download is a repeated four-bar demonstration; your eight-bar composition is the assignment.
