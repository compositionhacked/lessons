## Hear the device

A2 stays in the bass. Above it, C♯4–E4–A4 becomes D4–F♯4–A4. A4 stays; the other two voices rise. D/A is IV over the tonic bass, not a root-position D chord.

This original teaching example is 122 BPM in 4/4, 47.213 seconds, 24 bars. It illustrates a transferable device rather than transcribing or explaining the whole recording. Notes use scientific pitch notation (middle C = C4, MIDI 60). The chromatic pitch display uses C♯, E♭, F♯, G♯ and B♭ spellings; the chord labels and explanation give their harmonic functions.

## Write with it

Write eight bars on A2. Alternate A and D/A above it. Give the melody one short repeated cell; use no E chord.

Record your completed exercise, then remove or reverse the device and compare at the same master gain. The 24-bar demonstration includes explanatory repeats and a four-bar workshop preview. Your composition is a separate assignment.
