# Lesson 034 — Change One Note for a Borrowed Chord

**Goal:** Keep a lead coherent across a borrowed ♭VII chord by changing one letter of the key collection.

**Lesson URL:** https://github.com/compositionhacked/lessons/tree/main/034-one-letter-borrowed-chord

**Source:** CompositionHacked's *Fire Garden, Part II: Soloing and Modal Harmony* study, Rule 1 and original Example A. The study analyzes how *Brother* keeps home-key notes over a borrowed B♭ chord; this G-major illustration is original.

**Harmony and notes:** G major → F major → G major. Start with G A B C D E F♯. F major supplies F natural, so change F♯ to F for that chord only. The resulting F-root view is F Lydian: F G A B C D E.

**Demonstration:** Play B–A–G–F♯–G–E–D over G; F–G–A–B–C–B–A over F; resolve to G. B over F is the ♯11, used in motion. F, A and C are stable landing notes.

**Eight-bar assignment (separate from the demo):** Program G | G | F | F | G | G | F | G. Make a first pass using chord tones. On the second pass, cross B over F as a passing tone. On a third pass, improvise while changing only F♯ ↔ F at the chord boundaries. Check that F♯ does not linger over F and F natural does not linger after G returns.

**Demo timing and assets:** 88 BPM, 4/4. The short MIDI demo spans seven total bars: one count-in and six musical bars (about 19.09 seconds) and presents the pictured three-bar phrase with repeated/held bars. The three-bar guitar tab is in Example A.
