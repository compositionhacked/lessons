# Lesson 013 — Make the offbeat whisper.

## Hear the device

Bass E2–B2 alternates on numbered beats. The E3–G♯3 dyad sits on the & after each beat. A puts bass and dyad together; B moves only the dyad half a beat later. Both use the same short plucked sound and note strengths.

This original teaching example is 100 BPM in 4/4, 57.600 seconds, 24 bars. It illustrates a transferable device rather than transcribing or explaining the whole recording. Notes use scientific pitch notation (middle C = C4, MIDI 60). The chromatic pitch display uses C♯, E♭, F♯, G♯ and B♭ spellings; the chord labels and explanation give their harmonic functions.

Empty parts are intentional. All notes in the MP3 occur in this MIDI, including the guide when present; no extra backing or hidden drone is added. Assign a short electric-bass sound, clean plucked harmony, a clear lead and a contrasting guide sound. The demonstration uses simple additive/plucked synthesis; General MIDI program hints do not reproduce its exact timbre.

Exact onset beats, duration in quarter-note beats, MIDI pitches, velocities and part names are in [note-events.json](./note-events.json). Every release is clamped before the 96-beat ending. Repeated short bass notes are separate attacks, not a sustained pedal waveform. Where a tonic pedal is described, it means the repeated bass pitch stays fixed.

Play at the specified octave. Standard guitar tuning is E2 A2 D3 G3 B3 E4, with no capo in these newly composed examples. Bass and high keyboard parts can be recorded separately; this is an arrangement score, not a claim that one guitar plays all tracks simultaneously. Keep the master gain unchanged between A and B.

## Write with it

Write eight quiet bars: E–E–C♯m–C♯m | A–A–B–E. Use bass on beats and small muted dyads on offbeats. Save D♯ for B before the final E.

Record your completed exercise, then remove or reverse the device and compare at the same master gain. The 24-bar demonstration includes explanatory repeats and a four-bar workshop preview. Your composition is a separate assignment.
