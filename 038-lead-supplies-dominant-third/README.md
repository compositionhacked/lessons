# Lesson 038 — Let the Lead Turn v into V

**Goal:** Let the lead determine the third of a thirdless v chord in a minor key, turning v into a strong V.

**Lesson URL:** https://github.com/compositionhacked/lessons/tree/main/038-lead-supplies-dominant-third

**Source:** CompositionHacked's *Fire Garden, Part II: Soloing and Modal Harmony* study, Rule 4, Phrygian dominant playbook and original Example E. The study reports leading tones over v power chords in *There's a Fire in the House* and D♯ over B7 in *The Crying Machine*.

**Harmony and notes:** Am → E5 → Am. E5 contains E and B but no G or G♯. A natural minor gives E F G A B C D; changing G to G♯ creates A harmonic minor, read from E as E Phrygian dominant: E F G♯ A B C D.

**Demonstration:** Over Am, A–C–B–A–G–E. Over E5, E–F–G♯–B–G♯. Resolve to A over Am. F is a passing ♭9 over E; B–G♯–A encloses the arriving tonic.

**Eight-bar assignment (separate from the demo):** Program Am | Am | E5 | E5 | Am | Am | E5 | Am. Record one learner take with G natural over E5 and a second with G♯, keeping all other notes and the backing fixed. Then add F as a brief neighbor and finish B–G♯–A. Identify which take creates the stronger dominant pull and why. The supplied demo contains only the G♯ version.

**Demo timing and assets:** 94 BPM, 4/4. The short MIDI demo spans seven total bars: one count-in and six musical bars (about 17.87 seconds) and presents the pictured three-bar phrase with repeated/held bars.
