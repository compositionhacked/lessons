# 002 — Three chords. One shared note.

Learn to connect different triads by holding one voice and moving the others by semitone. This is an original exercise adapted from Lesson 6 of the Fire Garden composition study.

## Downloads

- [MP3 demonstration](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/compositionhacked/lessons/main/002-smooth-chords-one-shared-note/002-demonstration.mp3)
- [Editable MIDI](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/compositionhacked/lessons/main/002-smooth-chords-one-shared-note/002-demonstration.mid)

48 seconds, 100 BPM, 4/4. MIDI format 1, separate bass and clean guitar tracks, with tempo and chord markers. Assign instruments in your DAW. The MIDI stores notes and timing, not audio; sounds will depend on your instruments.

## Follow the voices

| Chord | Lower upper voice | Middle voice | Shared upper voice | Bass |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| E | G#3 | B3 | E4 | E2 |
| C | G3 | C4 | E4 | C3 |
| C#m | G#3 | C#4 | E4 | C#3 |

E → C: hold E; move G# down to G and B up to C. C → C#m: hold E; move G and C up a semitone. Reverse the second move to return from C#m to C. These triads share an E; the whole progression need not belong to a single diatonic scale. Small movement in individual parts can connect contrasting chords.

## Guitar

Standard tuning, low to high: E upper triad `x x x 1 0 0`; C upper triad `x x x 0 1 0`; C#m upper triad `x x x 1 2 0`. These are upper voicings. A separate bass states E, C and C# roots in the demonstration.

## Write eight bars

Use two bars each of E → C → C#m → C. Hold E in the upper voice, move each other upper voice by one fret and change the bass to the chord root. Add a melody, then try a different rhythm or register. Judge the connection by ear.

The demonstration presents a quick comparison, isolates each voicing, then replays the changes. Its final section leaves you the exercise; it is not a complete eight-bar rendition of the assignment.
